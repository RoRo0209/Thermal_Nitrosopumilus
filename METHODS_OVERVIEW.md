# Selected analytical methods

## Lineage abundance and environmental context

Marine metagenomic profiles were used to characterize ammonia-oxidizing archaeal lineages. The *Nitrosopumilus* (NP) fraction was expressed relative to the combined NP and WCA signal as NP/(NP + WCA). NP includes LowT, HighT, and other NP-affiliated sequences. This quantity is distinct from HighT/(HighT + LowT), which describes composition within the two focal clades, and neither ratio is an absolute cell concentration.

Sample locations were linked to gridded environmental predictors for distribution modelling. Grid-derived environmental values represent the environmental product rather than direct measurements at the sampling time. Use of surface environmental grids does not, by itself, establish that every metagenome was sampled at the surface.

## Phylogenetic framework and genomic differentiation

A phylogenetic framework was combined with predicted thermal traits and habitat associations to identify the LowT and HighT clades. Phylogenetic signal and ancestral-state analyses were used to examine the evolutionary structure of these traits. Genome-based thermal predictions were distinguished from experimentally measured growth optima.

Genome architecture, protein composition, functional annotations, and ortholog distributions were compared between clades. Gene-family reconciliation provided a complementary account of evolutionary turnover. These analyses describe genomic differentiation; they do not directly measure ecotype-specific ammonia oxidation activity.

## Spatial, temporal, and population-level comparisons

Spatial gradients and coastal time series were used to examine ecotype abundance patterns in independent environmental settings. Community composition was compared with environmental variation. Population-genomic analyses examined allele-frequency differentiation and sequence variation, subject to coverage and sample-availability constraints.

Associations between ecotype identity, environmental conditions, and sequence variation were interpreted separately from evidence of causal physiological effects.

## Distribution modelling and climate projections

Random-forest regression related lineage abundance or composition to environmental predictors. The NP/WCA composition analysis and the ecotype-specific abundance analyses are separate modelling tasks and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Ecotype abundance models were applied to matched present-day and future environmental grids under a specified climate scenario. Spatial changes were summarized using consistent geographic domains and area weighting. Such projections are conditional on the environmental inputs, training-data coverage, and transferability of the fitted relationships.

Projected relative abundance is not a direct estimate of absolute population size or ammonia oxidation rate. Translating abundance into process rates requires additional evidence beyond these distribution models.

## Ammonia oxidation observations

Compiled marine ammonia oxidation observations were examined in relation to temperature and substrate availability. Process-rate records and metagenomic abundance profiles represent different measurements. They were not assumed to provide matched, ecotype-specific activity estimates merely because they occurred in similar environments.

This overview does not present a validated prediction of future global coastal ammonia oxidation rates.
